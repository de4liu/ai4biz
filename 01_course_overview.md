### Course Description

Artificial intelligence is no longer a technology story — it is a business transformation story. This course equips upper-division business students with the strategic frameworks, conceptual depth, and hands-on fluency to understand, evaluate, and deploy agentic AI in real organizational contexts. Students enter with prior experience using generative AI tools; this course accelerates past the basics to focus on where AI is going next: autonomous systems that plan, act, and coordinate without constant human direction.

Over seven weeks, students progress rapidly through advanced generative AI techniques and enterprise deployment models, then spend three full weeks on the core subject: agentic AI. Students will learn how AI agents perceive and act on their environments, how multi-agent systems coordinate complex tasks, how organizations design for human oversight and failure recovery, and how to evaluate agentic AI for real business deployment — from design through governance. The course culminates in a cross-major team capstone pitch presenting an agentic AI solution for a specific business sector.

The course integrates tools from the Google Gemini ecosystem (including multi-modal and AI Studio capabilities), no-code workflow builders such as Zapier, Make, and Relevance AI, and agentic platforms including n8n and Voiceflow. Students will also engage with the strategic and ethical dimensions of AI adoption — enterprise governance, vendor selection, security, responsible deployment, and workforce implications — preparing them for roles in which AI fluency is a baseline professional expectation.

This course is designed for students across all Carlson concentrations — Marketing, Finance, Accounting, SCO, HRIR, MIS, Entrepreneurship, International Business, and Business Analytics — and requires no prior technical or programming background.

### Course Rationale

The business landscape is undergoing a structural shift. Generative AI tools — large language models, image generators, and multimodal systems — have moved from novelty to operational infrastructure in under three years. Agentic AI, in which AI systems plan, take actions, and coordinate with other systems autonomously, is accelerating this shift further. According to McKinsey's 2025 State of AI report, over 70 percent of organizations are piloting or deploying generative AI in at least one business function. Roles in marketing, finance, HR, and operations now routinely require AI fluency as a baseline skill.

Yet most business graduates enter the workforce with only surface-level exposure — typically limited to using a chatbot interface. They lack the conceptual vocabulary to evaluate AI vendor claims, the workflow intuition to identify automation opportunities, and the strategic grounding to lead or contribute to AI initiatives. This course closes that gap. It is explicitly designed for business majors who will manage AI tools, commission AI projects, and make strategic decisions about AI adoption — not for those who will build AI systems from scratch. The goal is informed, confident fluency: the ability to understand what AI can and cannot do, translate business problems into AI solutions, and lead responsibly in AI-augmented organizations.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Explain** the foundational mechanics of large language models and generative AI systems — including tokenization, training, inference, and hallucination — using accurate, non-technical language appropriate for business communication.

2. **Distinguish** among AI paradigms (rule-based systems, predictive ML, generative AI, and agentic AI), identifying which paradigm is appropriate for a given business problem and what constraints each carries.

3. **Design and execute** no-code AI-assisted workflows using automation platforms, connecting AI outputs to real business processes in marketing, operations, finance, or HR without writing code.

4. **Evaluate** agentic AI systems — including multi-agent architectures, tool-using agents, and AI copilots — by analyzing their reasoning patterns, failure modes, and appropriate scope of autonomy within an organizational context.

5. **Assess** the strategic, ethical, and governance implications of AI adoption in a business context, including workforce impact, bias and fairness risks, regulatory considerations, and vendor evaluation frameworks.

6. **Apply** a structured business problem-solving process to identify, scope, prototype, and present an AI-assisted solution to a real organizational challenge using tools covered in the course.

### Required Tools and Platform Access

This course uses no-cost or student-tier access to the following categories of tools. No software purchase is required; all platforms have free tiers sufficient for coursework. Specific setup instructions and access links will be distributed in the first week.

**Conversational AI and Multimodal AI (Weeks 1–4)**  
The Google Gemini ecosystem — including Gemini (conversational AI), NotebookLM (AI-powered research assistant), and Google AI Studio (prompt and model experimentation) — serves as the primary AI interface for the first half of the course. Students will use these tools to understand how large language models respond to different types of prompts, how context shapes output, and how multimodal inputs (text, image, document) change AI behavior. All tools are accessible via a standard Google account. Students without a Google account will need to create one before the first session.

**No-Code AI Workflow Builders (Weeks 3–5)**  
Platforms in this category — including Zapier, Make (formerly Integromat), and Relevance AI — allow users to connect AI capabilities to other software systems (email, spreadsheets, CRMs, calendars, databases) through a visual drag-and-drop interface. Students will use these tools to build automated workflows that incorporate AI decision-making without writing code. Free tiers on all three platforms are sufficient for course assignments. Students will choose one platform to use for their capstone project.

**Agentic AI and Conversational Agent Platforms (Weeks 5–6)**  
Platforms in this category — including n8n (workflow automation with agentic logic) and Voiceflow (conversational agent builder) — enable the construction of multi-step, goal-directed AI workflows and dialogue-based agents. These tools represent the frontier of accessible, no-code agentic AI. Students will use these platforms to build and test simple AI agents that can take sequential actions, branch based on outputs, and interact with users. Free tiers or educational access will be confirmed at course start.

**Note on Tool Evolution:** The AI tool landscape evolves rapidly. The platforms listed above are current as of course launch; the instructor reserves the right to substitute equivalent tools if access, pricing, or functionality changes materially. The conceptual skills developed in this course — workflow design, agent architecture, prompt strategy, governance evaluation — transfer across specific platforms.

### Required Materials

This course does not use a textbook. The AI field moves faster than print publishing cycles allow. Instead, required readings draw from three sources:

1. **Assigned articles and reports** — A curated reading list of practitioner reports (McKinsey, Deloitte, NIST), academic summaries, and long-form journalism will be distributed via the course LMS each week. Readings average 30–45 minutes per session.

2. **Case studies** — Harvard Business School and MIT Sloan case studies on AI adoption, automation strategy, and AI ethics will be used in weeks 3, 5, and 7. Case PDFs will be distributed through the course LMS. If your institution charges for HBS cases, a small fee (typically $3–5 per case) may apply; the instructor will confirm before week 3.

3. **AI-generated and tool-generated readings** — In some weeks, students will use NotebookLM or Gemini to generate synthesized briefings on topics, then critically evaluate those outputs as part of the learning activity. This is intentional: reading AI-generated content critically is itself a core course skill.

**Recommended (Optional):**  
- *Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI* — Ethan Mollick (2024): The most accessible book-length treatment of working with AI for non-technical professionals.  
- *The Coming Wave* — Mustafa Suleyman (2023): Strategic and geopolitical context for AI's trajectory.
